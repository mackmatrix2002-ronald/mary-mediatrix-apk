Who we are

A willing response to a life of faith

We are members of the Mary Mediatrix of All Grace Montefaro Group, Imus Chapter. We come from different paths of service and gather in faith to pray, welcome the image of Mary into homes, and accompany families through prayer and fellowship. Membership is a personal and voluntary response; no one is compelled to join.

Our story

History of the Montefaro Group

A faith journey shaped by service, prayer, and a shared devotion to Mary Mediatrix of All Grace.

A note on this account — This summary reflects the members’ shared recollections and beliefs. It does not assign dates or names that have not been confirmed.
How the group began⌄

Some of the early members had already spent many years serving God. They came from different Catholic communities, including the Soldiers of Christ and groups devoted to Our Lady of Fatima, as well as other religious organizations. Others felt drawn to serve Mary Mediatrix after experiencing healing, peace, or greater calm in their lives.

Members describe joining as a personal calling, never the result of pressure or recruitment. At first, some simply welcomed the Marian image into their homes. A few members later recalled dreams or visions of a woman whose appearance they recognized in the image. They also remember events they could not readily explain. The group understands these experiences as signs of Mary’s guidance and shares them as members’ faith testimony.

After the image was found, members began bringing it from home to home. It became known to them as Mary Mediatrix of All Grace, and the home visits grew into a shared ministry of prayer and service.

The home visit and prayer gathering⌄

Usually, the image stays in a host home for one week. During October, it visits a different home each day. The host family is invited to pray the Rosary daily and may offer personal prayers before the image. At the end of the visit, members gather for a simple Mass or prayer service, followed by a prayer-over or healing prayer as appropriate. The group then says farewell and accompanies the image to the next home, where it is welcomed again.

Members offer these prayers in a spirit of faith and care. Any healing or spiritual experience is entrusted to God and understood according to the group’s Catholic faith.

Shared service and discernment⌄

Members serve according to their gifts: some lead prayers and the simple Mass or service; others assist with prayer-over, spiritual encouragement, hospitality, and the practical care of each visit. The group also travels to churches and other places when members discern an invitation to serve there.

At times, members report dreams or visions that they believe may point toward a mission. They discuss and pray about these experiences together before acting, seeking confirmation and guidance with humility.
