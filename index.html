The 1948 Lipa Apparition: A Story of Faith and Devotion

A historical overview of the reported events at the Carmelite monastery in Lipa, Batangas.

In 1948, reports of Marian apparitions at the Carmelite monastery in Lipa, Batangas, became an important part of Filipino Catholic devotional history. The reported witness was Teresita “Teresing” Castillo, then a young Carmelite postulant. Accounts describe a series of experiences beginning in September 1948, associated with a vine in the convent garden and reports of showers of rose petals.

The reported appearances

According to accounts preserved by devotees and writers on the Lipa events, Castillo reported seeing a beautiful lady dressed in white, with a rosary, and later identified the figure as “Mary, Mediatrix of All Grace.” The accounts describe calls to prayer, humility, penance, and consecration to the Blessed Virgin Mary. Reports of rose petals drew visitors and strengthened devotion among many people in the Philippines.

Devotion and the Church's historical judgment

The events also became the subject of ecclesiastical investigation and controversy. On April 11, 1951, a committee of Philippine bishops issued a negative judgment regarding the alleged supernatural character of the reported events. In 2015, a local declaration in favor of the apparitions was subsequently nullified by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in 2016, with the earlier negative judgment remaining in force. The reported apparitions therefore should not be presented as officially approved by the Catholic Church.

Whatever one's understanding of the historical reports, Catholics may reflect on the virtues Mary exemplifies in the Gospel: faith, humility, obedience to God, and pointing others toward Jesus. Marian devotion is properly centered on Christ, and prayer should always be in harmony with the teaching and guidance of the Church.

Historical note: This summary distinguishes devotional accounts from official ecclesiastical judgments. It is an introductory overview, not an official Church declaration or a substitute for the original documents.
